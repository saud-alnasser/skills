# Question

Does the ASD-STE100 licence let AEP ship the STE dictionary or the text of its
writing rules?

# Sources

- ASD, "Simplified Technical English" page,
  https://www.asd-europe.org/standards-specifications/simplified-technical-english/,
  read 2026-10-25. Primary.
- ASD, "FAQ Simplified Technical English (STE)",
  https://www.asd-europe.org/standards-specifications/simplified-technical-english/faq-simplified-technical-english-ste/,
  read 2026-10-25. Primary.
- STEMG FAQ, https://asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html. Primary, but the server
  returned HTTP 403 on 2026-10-25, so it was not read.
- Wikipedia, "Simplified Technical English",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English, read 2026-10-25.
  Secondary; it cites the specification itself for the copyright notice.

# Findings

1. **source**: the current issue is Issue 9. The ASD FAQ names "ASD-STE100
   Issue 9" as the latest free copy. Wikipedia says it was published in January
   2025. True of the page as read on 2026-10-25.
2. **source**: the standard is free to download. The ASD FAQ says "It is an
   open-source standard and is free to download" and "Always use authorised
   sources". The download goes through a feedback form on the ASD page.
3. **source**: ASD owns the copyright and the trade mark. Wikipedia quotes the
   specification: "Simplified Technical English, ASD-STE100, is a Copyright and
   a Trade Mark of ASD, Brussels, Belgium", and "All rights reserved".
4. **observation**: neither ASD page states a licence, and neither grants a
   right to reproduce, redistribute, or embed the dictionary or the rules in
   software. Searched for and not found.
5. **source**: size and limits. Wikipedia: about 900 approved words (its table
   says about 875), 53 writing rules, and sentences of at most 20 words in
   procedures and 25 in descriptions.
6. **interpretation**: "open-source" on the ASD page means free of charge. It
   is not a licence. With "All rights reserved" and no grant, the default of
   copyright applies.

# Conclusion

AEP has no right to ship the dictionary or to copy the text of the rules. AEP
may name the standard, say it is free from ASD, and state its own rules in its
own words. The name "ASD-STE100" is a trade mark of ASD, so AEP uses it only to
name the source, never as the name of an AEP artifact or command.

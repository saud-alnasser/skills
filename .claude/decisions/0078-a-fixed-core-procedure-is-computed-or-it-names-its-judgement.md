# A fixed-core procedure is computed, or it names its judgement

Stability was asked for by name: procedures should behave deterministically,
and the effort's two residual risks — a norm lost in conversion, an extension
point invented rather than needed — were both judged acts. Decided: whatever a
script can compute ships as a derived script step whose output the stage
quotes, and an irreducible judgement states its inputs and its one question —
the position report's computed/judged split (ADR 0067), made the rule for every
fixed-core procedure rather than one report's shape. Three applications:
conversion is manifest-driven, each inventoried norm guarded by a fire-checked
assertion, the mechanism proven by a seeded deletion before other conversions
run; extension points are derived from a variation census, never invented; and
a deviation carries its declaring release, so its age is computed and one
release without a disposition fails the audit.

## Consequences

The conversion frontier serializes behind the pilot — parallelism is spent to
buy proof, deliberately. And determinism has a floor worth stating: routing a
request and running a grill are judgement by nature, so the rule does not
promise a workflow without judgement — it promises that judgement never hides
inside a step a script could have owned.

# What the chunking constraint costs at chunk count, and whether ~20,000-character substitutions compose

Executed 2026-08-14 against `03f510f`, on this session's own harness — item 7 of `substrate/08`,
across two sessions. **Round 5 measured latency and got a figure this round could not reproduce**;
both accounts are recorded below because the disagreement is the finding.
[`whether-chunked-substitution-carries-a-row`](2026-08-14-whether-chunked-substitution-carries-a-row.md)
is the frozen round-4 account that opened both halves of this item.

Verified against: the running Claude Code harness on Windows 11, 2026-08-14.
Conclusion: **Successful.** Several ~20,000-character substitutions **do** compose in one body.
The overhead is **per command, not per byte**, and it costs **~1.6–1.75 seconds per chunk
boundary** — seconds, not the minute round 5 reported. **Round 5's ~16-second mean gap did not
reproduce and is recorded as an unexplained outlier.**

Consumed: `.claude/decisions/0089-the-row-is-delivered-the-query-is-filters-and-a-miss-is-a-fact.md`,
the chunking-constraint paragraph — substrate/08;
`.claude/tickets/substrate/issues/08-whether-retrieval-actually-beats-an-exact-read.md`,
"Item 7 — measured, and it corrects round 5" — substrate/08; and
`.claude/tickets/substrate/map.md`, "Notes" and "Not yet specified" — substrate/08.

## Hypothesis

Round 4 closed item 1's delivery question and left two gaps, both of which became item 7:

- **Composition.** ~20,000 characters is proven only as a *single* substitution and ~9,036 only
  in a *multi-chunk* body. The two do not compose by measurement — a per-body effect absent at
  72 KB of 9 KB chunks is not thereby absent at 72 KB of 20 KB chunks. ADR 0089 records ~20,000
  as a **goal rather than a rule** for exactly this reason.
- **Latency at chunk count.** Round 1 timed *one* command at 8 seconds and nothing had timed a
  body of several. Round 4 called this "the sharpest thing item 1 leaves open", because at that
  figure an eight-chunk row costs either seconds or a minute *at every stage entry* — and a row
  that takes a minute to arrive is not a row delivered at zero cost, which reaches ADR 0089's
  delivery half.

Round 5 ran the latency half and reported an eight-chunk body spanning **112,499 ms** with a
mean inter-chunk gap of ~16 s, concluding the minute side. This round was designed to run the
composition half — and, because `t4x20`'s gaps came back an order of magnitude smaller, to
re-run round 5's own probe unchanged as a reproduction test.

## Method

Four throwaway skills under `.claude/skills/`, generated by one script. Each chunk is a single
command emitting a numbered payload between two sentinels that **carry their own epoch
timestamp**, so start and end are read off the delivered text rather than observed from outside:

```
!`{ echo "C1-START-5182 T=$(date +%s%3N)"; ... ; echo "C1-END-5182 T=$(date +%s%3N)"; }`
```

| Probe | Shape | Total | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `probe-t1x9` | 1 × 9,057 | 9,057 | single-command floor (round 5) |
| `probe-t8x9` | 8 × 9,057 | 72,456 | past `/implement`'s unfiltered row, 69,563 |
| `probe-t4x20` | 4 × 20,057 | 80,228 | **composition at 20 KB, and the per-command/per-byte discriminator** |
| `probe-t1x20` | 1 × 20,057 | 20,057 | single-20k control — **deliberately not run** |

Every line is 100 characters and numbered continuously across the whole probe rather than
restarting per chunk, so a short chunk says both that it was cut and where, and a missing chunk
leaves a gap the survivors reveal. Static `GAP-n-5182` text sits between chunks and a
`MARKER-BEFORE` / `MARKER-AFTER` pair brackets the body: static text cannot be withheld by a
substitution limiter, so a missing gap would mean the *body* was cut where a missing chunk means
a *substitution* was.

Three points about what the design does and does not license:

- **`t4x20` is the discriminating probe.** It carries *more* total bytes than `t8x9` in *half*
  the commands. If overhead tracks bytes its span should exceed `t8x9`'s; if it tracks commands
  its span should be roughly half. The two hypotheses predict opposite orderings, which is what
  makes one run decisive.
- **The single-20k control was skipped deliberately.** Round 4's `probe-gamma` already measured
  exactly that payload; re-running it would spend context to learn nothing.
- **`probe-t1x20` was generated but never invoked**, so it contributes nothing here and is named
  only so the deleted probe set is fully accounted for.

**The substitutions were confirmed performed rather than assumed.** From inside the context a
performed substitution is indistinguishable from authored text, so this was checked at the
source: the probe bodies are 876–2,255 bytes and delivered 9 KB–80 KB, every payload line is
produced by a `printf` loop that appears in the body and in the payload nowhere, and **the
timestamps are generated by `date` at call time** — millisecond epoch values no author typed.

The probes were written in the previous session and invoked across two, so the session boundary
round 2 established as sufficient was satisfied throughout.

## Result

### 1 — Composition is confirmed: four 20,057-character substitutions deliver whole

`probe-t4x20` put **80,228 characters** through one body as **four** commands. Both sentinels,
all four static gaps, line numbering contiguous `0001`–`0800` with no skip at any boundary, and
**no wrapper, preview, or path anywhere**. The gap round 4 named — 20 KB proven only singly —
is closed at four chunks.

### 2 — The overhead is per command, not per byte

| | `t8x9` round 5 | `t8x9` this round | `t4x20` this round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| chunks × size | 8 × 9,057 | 8 × 9,057 | **4 × 20,057** |
| total bytes | 72,456 | 72,456 | **80,228** |
| total span | **112,499 ms** | **12,449 ms** | **4,891 ms** |
| sum in-command | 229 ms | 194 ms | 92 ms |
| overhead share | 99.8% | 98.4% | 98.1% |
| inter-chunk gaps | 20541, 20058, 20594, 12979, 17910, 18697, 1491 | 1482, 2024, 1569, 1577, 2495, 1550, 1558 | 1572, 1596, 1631 |
| mean gap | ~16.0 s | **~1.75 s** | **~1.60 s** |
| ms per byte | 1.553 | 0.172 | **0.061** |

`t4x20` carries **11% more bytes than `t8x9` in half the commands and finishes in 39% of the
time.** Byte count does not order these runs; command count does. The in-command figures say the
same thing from the other side: a 20,057-byte substitution costs ~23 ms and a 9,057-byte one
~24 ms, so **generating the payload is spawn-dominated and the bytes are nearly free.**

A per-command model fitted to `t4x20` — `span ≈ (n−1) × 1,600 ms + n × 23 ms` — predicts
`t8x9` at **11,382 ms** against **12,449 ms** measured, holding to **~9%**. The residual is the
gap itself running slightly longer in the 9 KB run (1,751 ms against 1,600), not a byte term.

### 3 — Round 5's figure did not reproduce, and this is the load-bearing result

The same probe, unchanged, on the same machine, ran **112,499 ms** in round 5 and **12,449 ms**
here — **9× apart**. Round 5's seven gaps averaged 16.0 s; this round's averaged 1.75 s. Round
5's own final gap was **1,491 ms**, a 13× outlier against its other six and squarely inside this
round's distribution — one run's outlier matching another run's entire distribution.

**The ~16-second gap is therefore not the intrinsic per-command cost.** What produced it is not
established: nothing was instrumented outside the harness, and this round can only report that
it did not recur.

### 4 — How few chunks carry a row, and what that costs

At the 20,057 characters now proven in combination, `/implement`'s unfiltered row of 69,563
characters fits in **four** chunks, against eight at ~9,036:

| Emission | Chunks | Predicted span |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~9,036 per command | 8 | ~12.4 s |
| **~20,057 per command** | **4** | **~4.9 s** |

Four is the proven minimum, not the theoretical one: the cap is bracketed at (20,036, 30,036]
and unpinned, so three chunks would need it pinned above 23,188 characters and no run has done
that.

## Limitations

- **The slow run is unexplained, and n=2.** `t8x9` has been run twice with a 9× spread. The fast
  run is corroborated by a differently-shaped probe through a fitted model; the slow one is
  corroborated by nothing. But two runs do not establish a distribution, and **nothing here
  identifies what made round 5 slow** — so it cannot be ruled out that the condition recurs.
- **Nothing outside the harness was controlled or observed.** Machine load, other processes, and
  disk state were not measured in either round. The per-command figure is what this machine did
  on this day, twice.
- **Chunk counts of 4 and 8 only.** No upper bound was sought, as in round 4. Whether twenty or
  fifty chunks stay linear is unknown, and the model is fitted to two points.
- **The model is descriptive, not mechanistic.** ~1.6 s per boundary is a measured constant with
  no account of what spends it. Nothing here says it is process spawn rather than harness
  bookkeeping, and a constant with no mechanism is a constant that can move.
- **Three chunks were not tried**, so the minimum-chunk figure rests on the proven size rather
  than on the cap.
- **Bytes versus characters is still not separated** — payloads are ASCII throughout.
- **`probe-t1x20` was never invoked**, so no single-substitution control was taken *this* round;
  the comparison to one command rests on round 5's `t1x9` and round 4's `probe-gamma`.
- **Version-bound.** One harness build, one machine, one day.

## Conclusion

**Successful**, on both halves item 7 asked.

**Composition holds.** Four 20,057-character substitutions deliver whole in one body, with no
wrapper anywhere. ADR 0089 records ~20,000 as a goal rather than a rule on the express ground
that it was proven only singly; **that ground is now removed.** Whether the ADR promotes it is
the user's call, taken in the `/design` run this file is handed to — but the reason for holding
back no longer applies, and the same row costs 4.9 s in four chunks against 12.4 s in eight.

**The cost is seconds.** Round 4 framed the open question as "the difference between seconds and
a minute at every stage entry", and round 5 answered *a minute*. Measured again, an eight-chunk
row costs **~12.4 s** and a four-chunk row **~4.9 s**. On round 4's own criterion this lands on
the **seconds** side — the opposite of what round 5 concluded, and it does not put ADR 0089's
delivery half in question.

**Round 5's account is not amended, and it is not withdrawn.** It is a frozen record of what that
session measured, and this file is the second run rather than a correction to the first. What
changes is which number a decision should carry: **the ~1.9-minute figure is unreproduced and
should not be carried into a decision**, and any healing of ADR 0089 or the map should cite the
range and the outlier rather than either run alone.

**One methodological note worth carrying.** Round 5 drew a load-bearing conclusion from a single
run of a timing probe, and the conclusion was wrong. The probes are cheap and the cost of one
repetition was ~18k tokens; the cost of not repeating it would have been an ADR amended against
an artefact. Timing findings on this map should be run at least twice before they move a
decision.

Not promoted. The code is deleted with the rest of the probe set.
